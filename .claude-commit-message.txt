the label he asked for reaches the letter

Jimmy, 15 October, listing four button labels:

  "not choose - schedule your shadow days, choose your tour time to -
   schedule your tour, choose your time to - schedule your time, send the
   link to - send schedule interest meeting/conversation link"

Three landed in 3c908c8e. The fourth did not, and only a render found it.

BUTTON_LABELS.interest_link_action was set to his words and shipped. It never
reached him: inquiry_staff_alert wraps the merge field in its own anchor, and
letter-html keeps an author's anchor text on purpose - "a link somebody wrote
an anchor around is a decision", argued out on 10 October when the opposite
rule turned a deliberately plain link into a button. The label was right in
the code and wrong in the inbox, and ONLY THE LETTER COULD FIX THE LETTER.

Migration 558 changes the anchor text. Applied and read back: the letter now
says his words, 557's skip button survived the edit, 556's phone line survived
it, and the subject did not move - all four checked in the migration's own
guards, because two changes to one letter in one evening is exactly when a
careless replace() eats the other one.

DELETING THE ANCHOR TO LET THE LABEL APPLY WAS THE FIRST IDEA AND THE WRONG
ONE. For an HTML body the buttons are made by buttonsForSoleAnchorParagraphs,
which promotes a paragraph whose only content is an ANCHOR. A bare merge field
is not one, so that route would have produced a naked URL where a button has
been since September.

The test shipped three hours ago recorded the fault as a passing expectation,
with a note saying it would invert when Jimmy said the word. He said the word.
It is inverted, and now asserts that the letter and BUTTON_LABELS agree.
