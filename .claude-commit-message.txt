feat(platform): a named staff address may be written to while families are not

WHY THIS EXISTS

Leesa Davis was hired at The Academy Virtual on 8 October. Her password
reset was sent, EMAIL_DIVERT_TO caught it, and it arrived in Jimmy's
inbox under the "[ NOT SENT TO THE FAMILY ]" banner. She could not sign
in, so she could not record her classes, her hours or her children.

No member of staff can be onboarded at all while every outbound message
is diverted. The protection exists to shield FAMILIES; locking out the
people who work here is collateral, not intent.

    EMAIL_DIVERT_EXCEPT=leesa.davis@theacademyvirtual.org

WHAT I AM OVERRULING, SAID OUT LOUD

divert.ts said this, in these words:

    "ONE CHOKEPOINT, NO EXCEPTIONS. [...] A rule with an exception list
     is a rule somebody will fall outside of, so there is no exception
     list."

That sentence is now gone and its reasoning is not. It was right. The
list is therefore as narrow as it can be made, and the file says so
where the old sentence stood, so the next person meets the argument
rather than the conclusion.

THE THREE RULES THAT KEEP IT NARROW

  EXACT ADDRESSES ONLY. No domains, no wildcards, no patterns. A rule
  like @theacademyway.org is one typo from matching a parent, and
  whole-domain rules are how these lists quietly become everybody.

  EVERY RECIPIENT MUST BE ON THE LIST. Not one of them - all of them. A
  staff notice addressed to a school leader AND a guardian is diverted
  exactly as before. This is the rule that matters: it is the only
  reason a family cannot be reached by riding along as the second name
  on somebody else's letter.

  THE REPLY-TO IS STILL STRIPPED unless it is itself on the list. Staff
  notices set reply-to to the GUARDIAN, so a leader can answer a family
  without leaving her inbox. Let an excepted message keep that header
  and the first reply typed goes straight to a parent - by the one route
  nobody thinks to check. That is the same reasoning the original
  reply-to removal was written with.

Unset or empty, nothing changes. Everything is diverted, as before.

IT FAILS IN THE SAFE DIRECTION

An address that does not parse is dropped from the list, which DIVERTS
that mail rather than releasing it. A message addressed to nobody is
never excepted - an empty recipient list would otherwise satisfy
`every()` and pass straight through, which is the one way a careless
`every` becomes a hole.

THE RETURN VALUE IS NOT null, AND THAT IS NOT A STYLE CHOICE

send.ts reads null from divertEmail as "EMAIL_DIVERT_TO is set but
unreadable" and refuses to send. Returning null for an allowed
recipient would not deliver her mail - it would throw it away silently
and log a malformed-setting error that is not true. So the excepted
path returns the params themselves.

AND THE LOG NO LONGER LIES

"[email] diverted" was printed for every send while the diversion was
on. A letter that now reaches the person would have been logged as
caught - and that line is the first thing anybody would check, so it
would answer confidently and wrongly. It reads "[email] delivered,
divert exception" on that path, and reports where the mail actually
went rather than where the diversion address is.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CHANGE

Nothing reaches a family. EMAIL_DIVERT_TO is untouched and still set.
The only mail that leaves is mail addressed solely to an address a
person typed into Vercel on purpose.
