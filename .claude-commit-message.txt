the day the screens stopped lying

Seven fixes, five of them found by a founder clicking through his own product
and asking why it looked wrong. Every one turned out to be a real fault behind a
plausible surface.

1. THE GUARD COULD NOT NAME THE DOOR

Heather Badger-Brown, School Leader, signed in with the correct password and was
told to "ask your school's admissions office for the sign-in address". She IS the
admissions office.

resolveTenantSignInHost read schools.organization_id — a uuid — and handed it to
BrandRegistry, which is keyed by synthetic text ids. Migration 226 says so in its
own header. A uuid could never match, so every school account fell through to the
vague wording and the named-address path had never once run.

Migration 356 bridges the two namespaces on the table that exists, filled from
the campuses themselves. The resolver tries the real uuid first and checks the
error rather than reading data blindly. Nina now gets "Sign in here instead:
academy.thejag.org".

2. A SCHOOL LEADER WAS LOOKING AT PAYROLL

Nina's sidebar carried Human Capital & Workforce, Executive Workspace, Mission
Control and the whole executive band. /dashboard/hr opens with teacher and
contractor pay per campus: $140,236 across 16 people.

349 was written to the requirement's own words — money, scholarships, funds,
funding, taxes — and denied exactly those. It does not deny `hr`. Payroll reached
her through the one door not called payroll.

357 denies HR and the executive surfaces. 358 goes further and matches money
words ANYWHERE in a key rather than as a prefix, because ssis.funding.view had
survived both. On its first run it also caught ai.finance and cloud.finance,
neither of which starts with "finance.".

Verified: nothing money-shaped left at allow; academic access untouched.

3. HER DASHBOARD WAS TITLED FOUNDER MORNING BRIEF

The module at /dashboard is named that, and the Sidebar renamed it for
non-founders while TopNav rendered the raw one. Same screen, same person: sidebar
said "Home", header said "Founder Morning Brief". She never saw the founder's
numbers — only the name was his, which is arguably worse.

moduleForViewer() now does it once and both surfaces call it.

4. 94 FAMILIES WITH NOBODY FOLLOWING UP

Started from "why has The Academy FL never had a single admissions task". FL is
not special — it has five active leads and all five had nobody.

createStageAutomatedTasks() runs only from transitionLeadStage(). Imports, hand
SQL and the public form write lead_stage directly and never call it. 316 was a
broom; between it and today GA alone accumulated 20 more.

359 sweeps ~69. 360 is the door: a trigger on lead_stage, the same hook 062 has
used correctly for months, with the map in a table so it can be read and diffed —
and a test that reads the seed out of the migration and compares it to the
registry. 361 adds appointment reminders anchored one day BEFORE the date rather
than after the booking.

361 surfaced 28 families whose stage claims an interview, shadow day or tour that
exists nowhere in the schedule. They now carry a task saying exactly that.

5. AN INQUIRY IS NOT AN APPLICATION

The public form asked six required mailing-address questions that NOTHING in src/
has ever read. 362 removes them; the form ends at Preferred contact method.

And the button said "Submit Application" while the thank-you email those same
families receive calls it an inquiry. Both halves now say inquiry. The label
lives in two places and reverting one fails the test.

6. TWO REPOSITORY-WALKING TESTS HAD NO HEADROOM

studio-foundation passes alone in 36.4s against a 90s timeout and exceeded it
inside the full suite. Raised to 240s; js005 to 300s, matching what
js003-governance already uses. No assertion touched, no baseline updated.

WHAT THIS DAY KEPT TEACHING

Prefer the query that returns a value over the reasoning that returns a story.
Three theories died today — a stale deployment that was current, machine load
that was a timeout, a broken stylesheet that was browser zoom. Each cost a round
trip that a measurement would have saved.

And: rules written to the words of a requirement miss the thing the requirement
was about. Money under the word "HR", funding under the prefix "ssis.", an
application that was an inquiry.
