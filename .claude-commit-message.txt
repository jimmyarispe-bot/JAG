You can change what the form says, without a migration and without touching what it means

Ship two of the form builder. /dashboard/admin/forms now opens a working
draft, lets Jimmy or Danni edit every piece of text on the interest form, and
publishes it. The form is on version 22 because twenty-two migrations were the
only way to change a sentence; that ends here.

WHAT MAY CHANGE: the form title, section titles and descriptions, question
labels, help text, placeholders, and the LABELS on choices.

WHAT MAY NOT, AND WHY IT IS ENFORCED TWICE: a question's key is what every
recorded answer hangs off, and an option's value is what the answer IS.
Rename either and a year of submissions quietly stops meaning anything -
invisibly, because nothing errors. applyTextEdits can only touch text by
construction, and onlyTextChanged proves it again before the row is written;
a disagreement between them refuses the save and names the structural thing
that moved. 22 tests, pure, no database.

Nothing reaches a family until Publish. Saving writes to the draft; the public
form keeps serving the published version. Publish saves first, so what goes
out is what is on the screen rather than the last thing saved.

Migration 432 makes the publish one transaction. The unique index allows one
published version per form, so publishing is a swap - archive the old, promote
the draft, move the pointer - and doing that in three statements from the
application leaves a window with NO published version, where the public
inquiry form returns nothing to every family. The function is security definer
and executable only by service_role, so authorization stays in one place: the
FORM_BUILDER_ACCESS gate on the screen.

Discarding deletes the draft rather than archiving it. An archived version is
one families answered; a discarded draft was never in front of anybody, and
filing them together would make the history mean two things.
