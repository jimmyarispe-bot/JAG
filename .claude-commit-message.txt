fix(finance): whose rate it is, and a box that remembers her hours

Two faults in 002b12a5, found by review within the hour it shipped. Both
were dormant for as long as no hourly box rendered anywhere. That commit
made them render.

1. ANY TEACHER COULD CLAIM ANY RATE

setHourlyClaimAction asked whether the rate EXISTED:

    const rate = RATE_BY_KEY[rateKey];
    if (!rate) return { error: "That is not an hourly rate this platform knows." };

and nothing else. Not whose rate it is.

CRAIG_MANN_IVY_ASH_TUTORING is $30.00 an hour with weeklyHourCap null. From
002b12a5 until this commit, any of thirteen teachers could have posted
craig_mann_ivy_ash_tutoring with four hundred hours and put a well-formed
$12,000 line on Jimmy's payroll screen, under "1:1 tutoring with Ivy Ash".

The picker would never have offered it. A server action is not a picker: a
rate key is a string in a form submission.

WHY ROW-LEVEL SECURITY DID NOT CATCH IT. Migration 462's policies let a
teacher write any row on her own open week. RLS establishes WHICH WEEK is
hers and has never had an opinion about which RATES she holds. myOpenWeek
answers the first question. Nothing answered the second, in code or in the
database, and the check therefore has to live here.

A RATE SHE NO LONGER HOLDS BUT HAS ALREADY CLAIMED STAYS EDITABLE. Taking
somebody off RATE_KEYS_BY_EMPLOYEE must not freeze hours already entered
under it - that leaves a week carrying a figure its owner cannot correct and
nobody can reconcile. One extra read, only on the key that is not hers.

HOW LONG IT WAS EXPLOITABLE. About ninety minutes, and teacher_hourly_claims
is empty, so nothing was taken. It was never exploitable before, for the
reason the earlier commit set out at length: the box had never appeared, so
no teacher had any reason to know a rate key existed.

2. THE BOX OPENED AT ZERO AND DELETED HER HOURS

HourlyRow held useState("0") and was never given the saved figure. The row
had never rendered, so this had never been met.

    Monday    Katie enters 8 hours. Saved. Week reads $160.00.
    Wednesday She reopens the week. Total still says $160.00.
              The box says 0.
              She clicks into it to check, and clicks away.
              onBlur posts 0. setHourlyClaimAction DELETES the row.
              $160.00 gone, behind a success message.

Or she reads the 0, assumes the field is per-visit, and types Wednesday's 4
over Monday's 8 - the upsert on (teacher_week_id, rate_key) replaces rather
than adds, and she is $80 short with nothing to see.

THE SAVED HOURS NOW COME DOWN WITH THE WEEK. LoadedWeek carries
hoursByRateKey, built from the hourly rows loadTeacherWeek ALREADY reads -
no new query. page.tsx puts the figure on each rate and the field opens on
it.

WHY NOT READ IT OFF THE VIEW. teacherWeekView flattens an hourly claim into
a display row - a label and the words "8 hours" - which is right for reading
a week and useless for refilling a field. Parsing a number back out of a
sentence is how the next one of these gets written.

KEYED ON THE SAVED VALUE, so a figure that changes underneath - her own save
and the refresh after it, or a second tab - resets the field instead of
being held off by stale state.

ExtraRow has the identical shape and is NOT fixed here. Those boxes have
rendered for weeks, so it is a standing fault rather than one this pair of
commits created, and it deserves its own change and its own test rather than
being swept in at midnight.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT FIX, and what the same review found:

Katie Vetere's admin hour is defined FOUR times.

    work_pay_rates  admin_hourly   $25.00  from 1 Sept   Katie Vetere
    work_pay_rates  admin_hour     $25.00  from 21 Sept  Katie Vetere
    weekly-work.ts  admin_hourly   $25     max 10 hours a week
    rates.ts        katie_vetere_admin  $20.00  cap 20 hours

Two rows in one table under two codes, a third in the superseded 41-slot
model, and 002b12a5 added the fourth. work_pay_rates already solves this -
nullable employee_id, versioned by effective_from, exactly as migration 377
set out and 379 extended to class rates. PersonalRate has no effective_from,
so editing the constant silently reprices every period that reads it.

That is the real fix and it is not a midnight change. These two are, because
one of them is a hole and the other eats a teacher's pay on a Wednesday.

Typecheck clean. No test references LoadedWeek, loadTeacherWeek,
hourlyRates, setHourlyClaimAction or HourlyRow, so nothing moved with it.
