THE CALCULATION WAS NEVER WRONG

Renee Tracewell, 3 October:

    "I guest taught Marisa Vanella's class on October 1st at 11am, I put
     those 3 students in my scheduled class, but the pay didn't add up
     correctly for Thursday. Total for being a guest teacher: $45 Total for
     having my class is $40. This system only gave me $55, it should have
     been $85 Total payment should b $550 for the week."

Her 1 October 11:00am row: five students, $55, "Scheduled class".

Structured Literacy pays $35 for the first student and $5 for each one
after. Five children in one session is $55. Every other row in her week fits
the same rule - one student $35, two $40.

What she expected was two classes: her own at $40 and the cover at $45. The
difference is $30, which is exactly $550 - $520. Her arithmetic was right.
The JAG's was right. They were answering different questions.

JIMMY'S RULING, 8 October: ONE CLASS. $55 is correct. A teacher who combines
a colleague's children into her own session taught one class and is paid for
one class.

SO THE FIX IS A SENTENCE, NOT A FORMULA

Nothing on that screen told her the rule. She could tick five children, read
"$55", and still go home believing she was owed $85 - so she reconstructed
her own week at the kitchen table, got a different answer, and wrote to the
CEO about $30 she was never owed.

The register now says, while she is still choosing:

    5 students · $55.00 for this class. If you took another teacher's
    children into it, tick them here too — it is still one class and pays
    once, for everyone on it.

It is in the one place where somebody would otherwise get it wrong: open at
the moment she is adding the second teacher's children, carrying the live
amount as she ticks.

WHY THE AMOUNT AND NOT ONLY THE RULE. A rule in a sentence is something to
remember. A figure that moves as she ticks is something she can check. Renee
did not misunderstand the rate - she never saw the arithmetic happen.
