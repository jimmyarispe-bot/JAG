fix(admissions): a mother's inquiry stops running the school's nightly jobs

STEP 1 — THE PUBLIC INTEREST FORM at apply.theacademyway.org.

WHAT WAS HAPPENING

dispatchAdmissionsAutomation ended with:

    await processAllPlatformQueues(supabase);

That is the NIGHTLY RUNNER. It budgets itself RUN_BUDGET_MS = 45_000 and
works through thirty-odd jobs — KPI snapshots, executive insights,
compliance sync, and the heavy integration syncs for Google Workspace,
Microsoft 365, EDI and the rest. vercel.json schedules it at 0 0 * * *,
8pm Eastern, for exactly that reason.

The public interest form reaches that function:

    submitPublishedInterestForm       (interest-form/submit.ts:574)
      -> onInquirySubmitted           (communications/triggers.ts:229)
      -> dispatch                     (communications/triggers.ts:147)
      -> dispatchAdmissionsAutomation (automation/dispatch.ts:31)
      -> processAllPlatformQueues     (automation/dispatch.ts:79)

and hands it the SERVICE ROLE client (interest-form/submit.ts:454).

So every mother who filled in the form on the website ran the school's
entire nightly job runner inside her own submission, with full
privileges, and waited for it to finish.

THE EVIDENCE

admissions_leads.created_at is written at the start of a submission.
admissions_interest_submissions.submitted_at is written at the very end.
The gap between them is what the family sat and waited.

Fourteen submissions, 30 September to 7 October 2026:

    Logan Astor              07:33:18 -> 07:34:08    50s
    Logan Astor              07:34:48 -> 07:35:37    49s
    Ella Rose Douet Reeves   11:13:03 -> 11:13:52    49s
    Ian Xavier Matos Ortiz   19:29:38 -> 19:30:26    48s

Every one between 47 and 51 seconds. That is not variance. That is the
45-second budget plus overhead, measured fourteen times.

WHAT IT COST A FAMILY

Logan Astor's mother pressed submit three times — 07:14, 07:33 and
07:34 on 7 October. The Academy Virtual now holds three lead rows for
one child, with three full answer sets, and Heather Brown received
three new-inquiry alerts for the same boy.

Nothing crashed. The form came back every time. It simply took fifty
seconds to do it, with nothing on the screen to say so.

WHY REMOVING THE LINE COSTS NOTHING

Every letter this event sends has already gone out before that line is
reached. notifyAdmissionsEvent drains the communication queue itself at
communications/triggers.ts:59, near the top of the same call. The line
sent no letter, raised no notice, and did nothing at all for the family
whose request was paying for it.

The 8pm cron still runs every one of those jobs, unchanged.
/api/admissions/process-communications still drains the communication
queue every five minutes. Nothing stops happening. It stops happening
on a parent's click.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT FIX, SO NOBODY MISTAKES IT FOR FINISHED

- The three Logan Astor lead rows are still in production. Closing the
  first two is a separate migration, and a school leader's decision.
- The public form still has no guard against a second identical inquiry
  from the same guardian. detect_admission_duplicates already exists in
  admissions/duplicates.ts and is unused on that path. Separate work.
- The eighteen nightly failures are unchanged and were never part of
  this: six real timeouts (compliance.missionControl,
  ssis.disengagedFamilies, four kpi.snapshot jobs) and twelve syncs
  starved behind them. That triage is documented at
  platform/automation/process-queues.ts:465 and the decision there is
  still open.

THE RULE THIS WRITES DOWN

A request a family is waiting on does that family's work and no one
else's. Platform-wide work belongs to a schedule.
