A plan knows whether its own figures are final

Jimmy, 27 September: hold the contract until every figure is final. A plan
could not say that about itself. `status` answers a different question - is
this the current plan or one it replaced - which is why the unique index reads
`where status = 'active'`. A plan can be the active one and still be waiting on
a GA GOAL award nobody has decided. Two facts, two columns.

`lifecycle` is added beside `status`, which is left exactly as it was:

  draft                          being built; figures may still move
  awaiting_scholarship_amounts   every figure final but one; awaiting_reason
                                 names which
  final                          every figure final, no contract sent yet
  contracted                     contract sent and signed
  withdrawn                      the family left mid-year
  imported                       predates this column; came from a document,
                                 and does not claim to have been signed

Widening the existing `status` check was the other option and would have been
wrong. The late fee views, the insurance views, the balances view and the plan
editor all read `where status = 'active'`. A plan moving to 'final' would have
silently dropped out of every one of them. Rows quietly missing from a money
view is the failure this system keeps producing.

Refused by the database: awaiting without naming the figure it waits on;
'final' or 'contracted' with nothing to bill from - a scheduled plan needs
remaining_due and billing_basis, a month-to-month plan needs monthly_amount
(279 dropped NOT NULL from those, which is right for a draft and wrong for
anything a contract rests on); 'withdrawn' with no date.

A trigger stamps lifecycle_changed_at only when the lifecycle actually moves,
so an unrelated update does not reset the clock a decision queue reads from.
New view plans_waiting_on_a_figure: which child, which campus, what is missing,
how many days.

The 78 existing plans become 'imported', not 'contracted'. They came from PDFs
and there is no record of which were signed. The withdrawal clause and the late
fee chain both rest on a signed contract; the data will not assert one exists.

Twelve assertions run against a local Postgres 16 before this was sent:
awaiting without a reason refused, a stale reason on a finished plan refused,
final without a figure refused, contracted holding the same requirement,
withdrawn without a date refused, an invented lifecycle refused, the clock
holding still on an unrelated update and moving on a real one.

The scholarship half of this - 446, an award that can be applied for before its
amount exists - is a separate commit. This message originally described both;
only 445 was staged. Amended so the record matches the commit.
