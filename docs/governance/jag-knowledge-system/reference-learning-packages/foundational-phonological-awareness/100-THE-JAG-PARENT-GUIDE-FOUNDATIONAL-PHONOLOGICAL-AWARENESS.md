## 1. Plain-Language Explanation

### What Is Phonological Awareness?

**Phonological awareness** is your child's ability to hear and play with the **sound structure of spoken language** — without reading letters.

Think of it as **ear training for words**:

- Hearing that a sentence is made of separate **words**
- Hearing that a word like "butterfly" has **parts** (syllables)
- Playing with **rhymes** (cat / hat)
- Splitting words into **beginning and ending chunks** ( /c/ + at → cat)

This happens through **listening and speaking** — clapping, tapping, rhyming, and talking. **No flashcards required.**

### What It Is Not

| Not This | This Instead |
|----------|--------------|
| Learning letter names | That comes later — separate skill |
| Reading books independently | Listening to read-aloud supports PA — different goal |
| A test of intelligence | A teachable skill with practice |
| Only for preschoolers | Any age learner can build PA with the right practice |

### Where Your Child Is on the Journey

Your child's teacher tracks **24 small skills** grouped from "hearing words in sentences" to "ready for the smallest sounds in words." You may see plain-language updates like:

- "Working on clapping syllables in words"
- "Playing rhyme games"
- "Putting word parts together orally"

Ask your teacher: **"What PA skill is my child working on this week?"**

## 2. Why It Matters

### For Your Child

| Benefit | Real-Life Connection |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Reading foundation** | Strong PA is one of the best early predictors of reading success |
| **Spelling later** | Hearing word parts helps with spelling down the road |
| **Confidence** | Sound play is fun — builds love of language |
| **Multilingual strength** | Rhyme and syllable play in **any language** helps — not confusion |

### For Your Family

- **5–10 minutes** of sound play at home can reinforce school learning  
- You don't need special materials — rhymes, songs, and conversation work  
- Your notes help teachers see how skills transfer to home  

### Academy Way Philosophy

We don't wait for children to "grow into" reading readiness. If there's a gap, we **teach explicitly** — at any age — with evidence and celebration, not labels.

## 3. Home Activities

*Capacity-aware: 5–10 minutes · 3–5 times per week when assigned · stop before fatigue.*

### Activity 1 — Syllable Clap Name Chain

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Best for** | Syllable segmentation (school skills PA-007+) |
| **Time** | 5 minutes |
| **How** | Clap each syllable in family names: E-mma, Mi-chael, Gran-dma |
| **Extend** | Clap names of pets, favorite foods, characters in books |
| **Look for** | One clap per syllable — "Grace" = 1 clap, "Anna" = 2 |

### Activity 2 — Rhyme Time

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Best for** | Rhyme recognition and production (PA-011, PA-012) |
| **Time** | 5–10 minutes |
| **How** | Say a word; child says a rhyming word. Take turns. |
| **Examples** | "Say a word that rhymes with **log**." → dog, fog, hog |
| **Nonsense OK** | "bap" rhyming with "cap" counts — it's about sounds |
| **Look for** | Child generates rhymes without long pauses |

### Activity 3 — Sound Listening Walk

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Best for** | Attention to sounds; alliteration awareness (PA-018) |
| **Time** | 10 minutes on a walk or around the house |
| **How** | Notice environmental sounds; name them. Find things that start with the same sound. |
| **Examples** | "Bird and bus both start with /b/!" |
| **Look for** | Child notices or repeats sound patterns |

### Activity 4 — Rhythmic Read-Aloud

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Best for** | Sentence segmentation; rhyme exposure (PA-001, PA-011) |
| **Time** | 15 minutes |
| **How** | Read rhyming books with rhythm — pause so child fills rhymes |
| **Titles** | Any nursery rhymes, Dr. Seuss-style rhythm, poetry for children |
| **Look for** | Child finishes rhymes; taps along with rhythm |

### Activity 5 — Word Count Game

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Best for** | Sentence word awareness (PA-001, PA-002) |
| **Time** | 5 minutes |
| **How** | Say a short sentence. Ask: "How many words?" Child counts on fingers. |
| **Example** | "The dog runs fast." → 4 words |
| **Look for** | Correct count; multisyllabic words count as one word |

### Activity 6 — Blend the Parts

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Best for** | Syllable and onset-rime blending (PA-003, PA-014) |
| **Time** | 5 minutes |
| **How** | Say parts separately: "pen … cil" — child blends: "pencil!" |
| **Easier** | "sun … set" → sunset |
| **Harder** | "/m/ … op" → mop (onset-rime — when teacher says child is ready) |
| **Look for** | Quick blend without guessing from first part only |

## 4. Games

| Game | Rules | PA Skill |
|------|-------|----------|
| **Rhyme Relay** | Chain rhymes — each word must rhyme with previous | Rhyme production |
| **Syllable Freeze** | Dance to music; freeze and clap syllables of a called word | Syllable segment |
| **Odd One Out** | Say three words — two rhyme, one doesn't; child picks odd word | Rhyme discrimination |
| **Robot Talk** | Speak in syllable chunks; child blends | Syllable blend |
| **I Spy … Sound** | "I spy something that starts with /s/" | Alliteration |
| **Sentence March** | March and say one word per step in a sentence | Sentence segment |
| **Rime Swap** | "Say cat. Now change /at/ to /op/." → cop | Onset-rime manipulate |

**Keep it fun.** Stop while it's still enjoyable.

## 5. Conversation Prompts

| Prompt | What You're Building |
|--------|---------------------|
| "Can you clap the parts in your name?" | Syllable awareness |
| "What rhymes with **book**?" | Rhyme production |
| "Do **cat** and **hat** rhyme? Why?" | Rhyme explanation |
| "How many words in: **I love ice cream**?" | Word counting |
| "Say **cupcake** without **cup**." | Syllable deletion |
| "What sound do **sun** and **sand** share at the start?" | Alliteration |
| "Listen: **bas** … **ket** … **ball**. What word?" | Syllable blend |

**Tip:** Accept approximate answers during play — school probes use stricter criteria. Note struggles for your teacher.

## 6. Reading Routines

### Daily Read-Aloud (15 minutes)

| Element | Guidance |
|---------|----------|
| **When** | Same time daily if possible — bedtime works well |
| **What** | Rhyming books, poetry, songs — not decodable phonics drills |
| **Your role** | Model rhythm; pause for rhymes; clap syllables in favorite character names |
| **Child role** | Fill rhymes; repeat phrases; clap along |
| **Multilingual** | Read in home language too — PA transfers |

### Weekly Sound Play Session (5–10 minutes)

- One activity from Section 3  
- Log one observation in Parent Portal if assigned  
- Celebrate one success — "You noticed that rhyme!"  

### What to Avoid at Home

- Forcing letter drills before teacher introduces them  
- Long sessions when child is tired  
- Correcting every error — note patterns for teacher instead  
- Comparing siblings' progress

## 7. Signs of Progress

| You Might Notice | School Competency Area |
|------------------|------------------------|
| Child claps syllables spontaneously | Syllable segmentation |
| Finishes rhymes in songs/books | Rhyme recognition |
| Makes up silly rhymes | Rhyme production |
| Counts words in short sentences | Sentence awareness |
| Blends parts when you slow a word | Syllable / onset-rime blend |
| Notices when words "sound the same at the end" | Rhyme discrimination |
| Plays "robot talk" for fun | Syllable blend |
| Corrects own rhyme mistakes | Discrimination emerging |

**Progress is not always linear.** Plateaus are common — extra school practice usually helps.

## 8. When to Seek Additional Support

### Talk to Your Teacher When

| Sign | Why |
|------|-----|
| No rhyme interest after weeks of school PA + home play | May need different approach or dosage |
| Fatigue or frustration within 5 minutes consistently | EF or attention supports may help |
| Child cannot clap syllables in own name after extended practice | Placement review |
| You see progress at home but school shows stall | Share home logs — calibration helps |
| Child avoids all sound games | Motivation or anxiety — team approach |

### What Additional Support May Look Like

- More frequent short PA sessions at school  
- Smaller group or 1:1 for a period  
- EF accommodations (breaks, visual schedule)  
- Speech-language coordination if articulation affects clarity  
- **Not** a label or diagnosis from home observation alone  

### Escalation Path

1. Message teacher with specific observations  
2. Parent Coach (AI) may suggest activity — teacher sees all flags  
3. Conference if stall persists 3+ weeks  
4. Intervention team if Tier 2 plan proposed  

**You are part of the team.** Your observations inform — teachers validate mastery.

## 9. Frequently Asked Questions

### General

**Q: Is this dyslexia therapy?**  
A: No. PA instruction helps **all** learners and is especially important for children who struggle with reading. Only qualified professionals diagnose. We focus on teaching.

**Q: My child knows letters. Why still clap syllables?**  
A: Letter knowledge and PA are **different skills**. Both matter. PA is oral; letters come in alphabetic instruction.

**Q: We speak two languages at home. Should we only practice in English?**  
A: **Practice in both.** PA in any language builds brain pathways for language structure.

**Q: How long until my child reads?**  
A: PA is **foundation**, not reading itself. After PA foundation, school moves to smallest sounds (phonemes) and letters. Timeline varies — mastery-based, not calendar.

### Activities

**Q: My child gives silly nonsense rhymes. Is that OK?**  
A: **Yes** — nonsense rhymes show they hear ending patterns. That's valuable.

**Q: Should I correct every wrong answer?**  
A: During play, keep it light. Note repeated errors for your teacher. School uses structured probes.

**Q: How much time should homework take?**  
A: **5–10 minutes** when assigned. More is not better if child is tired.

### School Connection

**Q: What is L3 / mastery?**  
A: Your child meets clear criteria (usually 4 out of 5 trials) observed by the teacher — not just home practice.

**Q: Can my home log alone prove mastery?**  
A: **No.** Home logs supplement school evidence. Teachers verify.

**Q: What happens after PA?**  
A: **Phonemic awareness** — working with individual sounds in words — then letters and reading. Teacher will explain when ready.
